# Rebuilding your website without losing search visibility

A new website is often the right decision. But a rebuild can also undo years of search visibility in a single launch, if old pages disappear without a plan. The good news is that the risk is well understood, and most of it can be avoided with preparation.

## Know what you have before you change it

Before anything is redesigned, make an inventory of the current site:

- every URL that exists today
- which pages receive search traffic, and for which queries
- which pages other websites link to

Google Search Console shows the pages and queries that bring visitors. Keep this list. It is the basis for every decision that follows.

## Keep the content that performs

Pages that rank well usually do so because they answer a question clearly. When you rewrite them, keep the substance. Improve the structure and language, but do not remove the information that made the page useful.

If you merge several old pages into one, make sure the new page covers what each of them covered.

## Redirect every old URL that matters

When a URL changes, the old address should send visitors and search engines to the new one with a permanent (301) redirect. Without it, visitors land on an error page, and the visibility the old page had is lost.

Map each old URL to the most relevant new page. Avoid sending everything to the homepage. A redirect to a page that does not match the original intent helps no one.

## Plan new languages and markets carefully

Adding a language or market is a good moment to rethink structure. Use separate URLs for each language, tell search engines how the versions relate with hreflang tags, and keep one canonical address for each page. Do not redirect visitors automatically based on their location. Let them choose, and make the language switcher easy to find.

## Check the technical basics on launch day

- All redirects work and point to the right pages.
- The new sitemap lists only live, indexable pages.
- No important page is accidentally set to noindex.
- Page titles and descriptions are unique.
- Internal links point to the new URLs, not via redirects.

Then submit the new sitemap in Google Search Console.

## Watch the first weeks closely

Some movement in rankings after a launch is normal. Follow the pages that matter most, check for crawl errors and broken links, and fix problems quickly. Most issues found in the first weeks are easy to correct.

## A rebuild can improve visibility

Handled well, a new website often performs better than the old one: faster pages, clearer structure and better content give search engines more to work with. The key is to treat search visibility as part of the project from the first day, not as a check after launch.
