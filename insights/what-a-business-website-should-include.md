# What a business website should include

A business website has one main job: to help the right visitors understand what you offer, trust that you can deliver it, and take the next step. Everything on the site should support that job. Here is what that means page by page.

## The homepage: answer three questions quickly

Within a few seconds, a visitor should know:

- what you do
- who it is for
- what to do next

That usually means a clear headline that describes the offer in plain words, a short supporting sentence, and one primary call to action. Clever slogans can work, but not at the expense of clarity. Below the opening, give visitors a route to each main service and a reason to believe you.

## Service pages: one page per service

Each core service deserves its own page. It helps visitors find exactly what they are looking for, and it helps search engines understand what you offer.

A strong service page explains:

- the problems the service solves
- who it is for, and who it is not for
- what the client actually receives
- how the work is organised, step by step
- how to get in touch

Answer the questions your sales team hears most often. If customers keep asking the same thing on the phone, the answer belongs on the page.

## About: show who is behind the business

Visitors want to know who they will be working with. Explain where you are based, how you work and what you stand for. Be specific and honest. A short, concrete description builds more trust than a long list of adjectives.

## Proof: only what you can document

Case studies, client logos, testimonials and certifications are powerful, but only when they are real and permitted. If you are a young company without published cases yet, show your process and your own work instead, and add proof as it comes.

## Contact: make the next step obvious

The contact page should be easy to find from every page. Keep forms short and ask only for what you need to reply well. For international visitors, a field for country and a clear note on how and when you reply are useful. If you offer calls, a booking link saves several emails.

## Language and audience

If you serve customers in more than one market, decide early which languages the site needs. In the UAE, for example, Arabic is the official language and English is widely used in business, so many companies there publish in both. A multilingual site needs a structure that search engines understand, with separate URLs per language and correct hreflang tags.

## Speed, mobile and accessibility

Most first visits happen on a phone. Pages should load quickly, text should be readable without zooming, and buttons should be easy to tap. Clear contrast, descriptive links and logical headings help everyone, including people using assistive technology.

## A checklist before launch

- Each page has a unique title and description.
- Each service has its own page.
- Contact options are visible on every page.
- Forms work, and you receive the submissions.
- The site works on phones, tablets and desktops.
- Images have descriptive alternative text.
- A sitemap is submitted to Google Search Console.
